### Option A — The "Scale" Angle

Are you printing on generic blanks, or are you actually building a fashion brand?

When fashion startups launch, they face a very real decision:
A) Private Labeling (buying pre-made blanks and printing a logo on them).
B) Custom Manufacturing (cutting and sewing custom proprietary silhouettes).

Option A is great for content creators or testing a simple graphic. But the ceiling is painfully low. When you try to scale, those $12 premium wholesale blanks crush your margins, and you're left selling a product identical to your competitor's (just with a different graphic).

Option B is how true fashion houses are built. 
When you custom manufacture your hoodies—choosing the exact 350 GSM French Terry, the precise drop-shoulder angle, and a unique silhouette—your fit becomes your moat.

When someone asks your customer, "Where did you get that?" they can ONLY buy it from you.

### Option B — Educational Contrast

Private Label vs. Custom Apparel Manufacturing.
Do you know which approach is right for your clothing brand?

🚨 Private Label (White Label):
-> You buy pre-made blank T-shirts/Hoodies.
-> You print your logo on them.
✅ Pros: Fast to launch, very low MOQ.
❌ Cons: No unique identity, low margin ceiling, you don't own the fit.

🚨 Custom "Cut & Sew" Manufacturing:
-> You design the exact measurements from scratch.
-> Factory cuts the fabric and sews it specifically for you.
✅ Pros: Proprietary fit/silhouette, much better profit margins at scale, true brand equity.
❌ Cons: Takes longer to develop, higher initial investment, requires technical knowledge.

Which is better? If you're a fast merch brand, Private Label works. But if you want to build long-term legacy brand equity like Represent or Corteiz, you MUST transition to Custom Manufacturing.
