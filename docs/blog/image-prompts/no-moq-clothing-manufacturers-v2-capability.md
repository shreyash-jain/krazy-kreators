## Why v1 was rejected

Every frame in v1 depicted *absence*: an empty cutting table, one garment on an empty rail, a tee in a black void, a near-empty shop rail. No people, no hands, no work. It read as an anonymous content publisher's stock library, not as **Krazy Kreators — an established end-to-end manufacturing partner.**

Two causes: concepts were driven from "small batch = few units," which produced a visual language of emptiness; and the MASTER_PROMPT likeness rule (*no identifiable faces; hands/backs/silhouettes explicitly allowed*) was over-read as "no people at all." **It is a face rule, not a human rule.**

## The two rules for every frame

> **1. Show capability, not emptiness.** Every frame contains skilled work happening — hands mid-task, a line running, a garment measured, checked, pressed, packed. Small batch is shown by *quantity in frame*, never by an empty room.
>
> **2. It must look photographed, not generated.** Every prompt below specifies a real camera body, a real prime lens and a real film stock, plus the imperfections a real frame has: natural grain, available light only, a slightly off-level handheld horizon, one element softly out of focus, visible wear on tools and surfaces, real skin texture on hands (knuckles, veins, short unpolished nails, a chalk smudge).

**Still hard-banned:** identifiable faces to camera · real logos or brand marks · legible text · flat-lays of fabric swatches · anything glossy, symmetrical or "rendered."
**Encouraged:** hands, forearms, backs, over-shoulder, cropped-at-shoulder figures. This is what makes it look like a real company.

**Append to every prompt** (already included below): *warm neutral cast, natural available light, real 35mm film grain, slight handheld imperfection, depth layering. Small "KK" watermark bottom-right, low opacity. Not a render, not CGI, not over-smoothed, not AI-glossy, not a stock photo. No faces to camera, no logos, no readable text. Square 1:1.*
