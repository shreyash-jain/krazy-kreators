# CLIENT — Krazy Kreators

Who the client is, who reads them, how they sound, and every standing instruction they
have given. **This file is living.** Every time the client or the marketing lead reacts
to a draft, write the lesson down here as a rule with its reason. That is the whole
mechanism by which the next blog is better than this one.

---

## The business

**Krazy Kreators** — an end-to-end brand-building partner for US clothing founders:
design, sampling, premium fabric sourcing, retail-grade production, packaging. One roof,
one project manager. India-based manufacturing, US-facing market.

- Site: **https://krazykreators.com** (blog at `/blogs`)
- **Positioning line that must never slip:** a *partner*, not a vendor, factory, sourcing
  agent, or discount manufacturer. Anything that frames them as a supplier competing on
  price is off-brand.

## The reader

A US clothing-brand founder, roughly **25–45**. Smart, time-poor, already past the
beginner content. Reads to get a frame, a number, or a decision they can act on Monday.

- **US-only framing throughout.** Regulations, seasons, retail calendar, currency, tariff
  regimes — all US.
- Assume they have read none of the other posts: define every industry term on first
  mention with a 6–10 word parenthetical.

## Voice

A senior fashion-trade insider giving a peer-level briefing — a Bloomberg fashion
dispatch, not a YouTube how-to. Confident, never condescending. Authority comes from
evidence and specificity, **never** from telling the reader what they "probably don't
realise."

- Let lines land. Vary rhythm — short declaratives punctuating longer explanations.
- Reach for real industry register: *"the cost shows up twelve months in," "the math has
  flipped," "customers can tell," "the shelf goes to a competitor," "the window is open
  right now."*

### Banned words and phrases — never use, in any form

`in today's fast-paced world` · `ever-evolving landscape` · `unlock` · `leverage` ·
`synergy` · `transform` · `empower` · `seamless` · `revolutionary` · `game-changing` ·
`elevate` · `harness` · `it's important to note` · `let's dive in` · `in conclusion` ·
`buckle up`

Also banned: listicle openers ("Here are 5 ways…") and tutorial framing ("What is X",
"How to X").

### Byline

**Krazy Kreators Team** + a desk label — e.g. `· Production & Sourcing`, `· Culture
Desk`, `· Growth & Business`. Never a personal or persona name unless explicitly asked.

Meta row: **Krazy Kreators Team · [Desk] · [Date] · [Category] · [Read time]**

The internal desk clusters (Production & Sourcing / Culture & Brand / Growth & Business)
are for choosing a topic's angle — not for inventing an author.

## Commercial rules

- **Krazy Kreators appears only at the close.** Never mid-body.
- **No pricing, no MOQ, no "Zero MOQ", no "free sampling", no "dedicated project
  manager"** anywhere in the editorial prose. The commercial ask is its own block after
  the article ends.
- The closing prose never ends on a sales pitch — it ends on a question that invites a
  reply, or a short "what we'd do in your shoes."

## Visual direction

2026 editorial / cinematic — high-end lookbook crossed with *Bloomberg Businessweek*
photography. One cohesive colour treatment across all images in a post, plus a small
**"KK" watermark** in a corner.

**Hard bans — never generate any of these:**

- overhead flat-lay of folded fabric swatches or cloth squares on a plain surface
- small handwritten paper tags as a prop
- generic factory-floor / sewing-machine close-up clichés
- the same composition twice in one post, or anything that looks like a stock-site result

**Trademark and likeness:** no real logos, crests, brand marks, or identifiable real
people. Generic unbranded product; generic non-famous people, and faces only as hands,
backs, silhouettes or tight crops. *Exception:* if a real product or person **is** the
subject of the post, a licensed press/product photo is the only legitimate route — flag
the licensing call to the owner and never fake a real brand with AI.

## Standing instructions and past feedback

*Append every new one, newest first, with the date and the reason. Never delete an entry
— supersede it and say so.*

- **2026-08-26 — A word count the owner gives you is a hard ceiling, and you hit it by cutting whole
  sections, not by thinning every one.** Asked for "under 1000 words" on a 2,879-word draft, the fix was to
  delete three complete H2 sections and keep six intact — not to trim every paragraph by a third. *Why:*
  evenly-thinned prose reads choppy and loses the specificity that makes it credible; whole sections can go
  without the reader noticing a seam. Keep the teaching graphics when you cut — they carry dates and rules
  at almost no word cost, which is what lets a short post stay useful. Report the exact count back, and say
  what the FAQ adds on top so the number is honest.
- **2026-08-26 — "Human, easy and understanding" means plainer sentences, not a tutorial.** The owner asked
  for this on a dense regulatory piece. What worked: explaining the mechanism before naming it, converting
  every unit the reader does not think in (2.6 oz/sq yd → about 88 g/m²), defining terms inside the sentence
  rather than in brackets, and cutting compressed trade-desk constructions. What stays: the confident
  register, the specificity, the concession. It is not a licence for listicle or "what is X" framing.
- **2026-08-26 — Extract the primary-source PDF rather than trusting a summary of it.** Three key claims
  (the 16,290,891 apparel certificate estimate, the exact risk-score wording, and the "such as adult wearing
  apparel relying on 16 CFR 1610.1(d)" line) appear in *no* web summary and could only be confirmed by
  pulling text out of CPSC's own final-rule PDF. federalregister.gov and ecfr.gov both 302-redirect to an
  unblock page, so fetch the agency's own PDF and decompress its streams. *Why:* a figure that survives only
  in secondary coverage is a figure you cannot stand behind.
- **2026-06-30 — Byline is "Krazy Kreators Team", not a persona.** Reversed the earlier
  named-persona system. *Why:* the personas read as invented people, which undercut the
  trade-desk authority the blog is built on.
- **Standing — correct the brief when it's wrong.** Supplied topic packets have shipped
  wrong figures (a tariff spread and a de-minimis timeline both needed correcting).
  Verify against primary sources and fix them, then say what you changed.
- **Standing — every prescriptive piece concedes a counterexample or edge case.** A post
  that admits nothing reads as marketing.
